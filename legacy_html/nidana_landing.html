Why paper and spreadsheet
trials fail audits.

The three failure modes regulators cite repeatedly in Indian Ayurveda trials — none of which require bad intent. Just the wrong infrastructure.

📋

Post-hoc data entry

Observations recorded on paper and transcribed hours or days later cannot demonstrate contemporaneity. ALCOA+ requires attribution and timing — a spreadsheet cannot prove either.

"Investigator could not demonstrate the original record was not altered before transcription." — GCP-ASU inspection finding
✏️

Silent overwrites

Editing a cell in Excel leaves no audit trail by default. Even specialised EDC platforms often log changes in a secondary table that can be cleared or bypassed by administrators with database access.

"No evidence of an audit trail maintained for data changes; corrections not legible." — CDSCO inspection observation
🌿

Missing Ayurveda context

Generic EDC platforms have no fields for dosha assessment, prakriti phenotyping, dravya lot numbers, Panchakarma procedure parameters, or classical formulation ratios — so teams add free-text workarounds that destroy codability.

"Non-standard data fields prevent mapping to CDISC SDTM; dataset rejected by reviewer." — CTRI submission feedback

Each capability below is a first-class record in Nidana's audit log. Nothing is an afterthought; everything that happens in the system is sealed.

CAP-001
Provenance

Immutable blockchain anchoring

Every save event — consent sign-off, AE record, dosage update, milestone completion — is SHA-256 hashed and written to the EVM ledger within seconds. The database may be queried freely; the chain cannot be rewritten. Even Nidana administrators cannot alter history.

SHA-256 per entry Merkle batch every 5 min Polygon / Sepolia CTMSLedger.sol on-chain
CAP-002
Compliance

ALCOA+ enforced at point of entry

Attributability, Legibility, Contemporaneity, Originality, Accuracy — plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available — are checked before a record is committed. Missing fields, unrecognised values, or failed attribution block submission. Compliance is structural, not procedural.

Role-based attribution Server-side timestamp No orphaned entries GCP-ASU aligned
CAP-003
Ayurveda EDC

Native Ayurveda data model

Prakriti and vikriti phenotyping, dravya lot traceability with GACP batch numbers, Panchakarma procedure logs with karma type and duration, classical formulation dosage with anupana and kala — all first-class structured fields, not free text.

Dosha / dhatu assessment Dravya traceability Panchakarma protocols Anupana & kala fields
CAP-004
Export

One-click CDISC and HL7 FHIR export

Generate submission-ready CDISC SDTM v1.7 datasets and HL7 FHIR R4 bundles from any completed trial. Each export is hash-verified against the on-chain state before delivery — so reviewers can independently confirm the data has not been altered between capture and submission.

Safety

NPvCC pharmacovigilance integration

Adverse events and serious adverse events are captured in MedDRA-coded structured fields. NPvCC-compliant narratives are generated automatically. The full AE chain — detection, causality assessment, reporting — is sealed and attributable in the ledger.

Capture. Seal. Export.

Three steps. No manual intervention between clinical capture and chain-verified export.

01

Capture

A clinician enters an observation — dosha assessment, herb dosage, safety event — through a role-gated form. Fields are validated against the Ayurveda ontology before submission is allowed.

Entry payload (excerpt)
event: "DosageAdministered"
patient_id: "AIIA-PT-00314"
dravya: "Ashwagandha churna"
dose_mg: 3000
anupana: "warm milk"
kala: "bedtime"
recorded_by: "Dr. Mehra [INV-04]"
ts: "2024-11-18T09:14:32Z"
02

Seal

The record is written to the database with a server-side UTC timestamp. Every 5 minutes, new records are SHA-256 hashed, batched into a Merkle tree, and the root is committed to the EVM smart contract.

03

Export

At any point, the data manager requests a CDISC SDTM or FHIR bundle. Nidana re-hashes all records and verifies them against the chain before generating the file. The export itself is anchored to a new block, creating an auditable submission event.
